WaveWednesday: Team AI is up 3-1 and a Christmas song started it

Four battle nights, one main event, 19 quick battles, 15 winners, four tied quick battles, and about $370 put behind songs by people who were sure. On the team board it is Team AI 3, Team HumanZ 1, one tie, 59 days left to settle it. That is the scoreboard. This is the week.

How a night works, in one line

Two songs go up in the room. You back the one you think wins, and the price moves as people pile in. Three signals decide it: the poll, the chart (who the crowd backed with money), and DJ Wavy, our resident ear. Best two of three. The artists get paid straight from the action, not from a sponsor.

Wednesday 4 November: the vibe sesh

We opened the week with a confession. The battle analytics had gone down the night before because the cache came off the leaderboards and the app tried to pull 172 battles at once. CPU to the ceiling, database locked, a couple of recent battles missing for a bit. Fixed by the time the room opened. Then a side quest on Google Antigravity, the new coding tool we are using to rebuild the home page section by section, and two tips from the room that every builder should have tattooed somewhere: never push to main before you test, and when it breaks, go back to the last commit that worked.

The battles: Before the Applause by aporkalypse78 beat Young Buck by stiloworld. Ca$ino beat Original VS Replica, both from lui, so lui won either way. Lighthouse and Ca$ino fought to a tie.

Friday 6 November: five fights

Life Goes On by Cannon Jones feat. Premo beat Killing Floor by godcloud. Rudolph beat Dolphin Flippin by geekmyth. Lighthouse beat Original VS Replica. Repeating the Woes of the World beat Kiss of the Reaper by stormi. High That I Crave beat Get Well. Small money, big room, five decisions.

Sunday 8 November: the main event

Dr. Bruce versus Nessy the Rilla, best of three. Nessy took round one. Bruce took rounds two and three and the night, with about $72 backed across the three rounds. A main event runs on three signals too: a human judge, the X poll, and the people who put money down. The first two rounds split the signals. Round one, the judge went Bruce while the poll and the money went Nessy. Round two, the judge went Nessy while the poll and the money went Bruce. Round three, all three agreed on Bruce.

Wednesday 11 November: last night

The room was sponsored by Lit Studio and it opened with the biggest fight of the week. Humans versus AI, straight up: $PEPEWavez by Hurric4n3Ike against WaveWarZ, the electric vibez, Zaal's AI track. About $200 went through the charts in eight minutes, more than Sunday's main event across three rounds. The poll went 3-1 for the electric vibez, the chart went the same way, and DJ Wavy made it three for three: "With a confidence level of 80 percent, the winner is WaveWarZ, the electric vibez." Hurricane, after the dust: "This was all a shock, bro. I wasn't expecting all this calamity right off the jump." Then he thanked the room for the chart activity, because he caught some gains on the way down. Point to Team AI. Zaal, on air: "AI is in the lead, three to one."

Then four more. Enter the Meta Within by geekmyth and StarBound by stormi split the poll one each, geekmyth took the chart, DJ Wavy took StarBound, so it stands as a tie. Get Well (v5) beat Hidden in Plain Sight, both from bennyj504, two nil on the poll with Wavy agreeing. MOONZ beat Earth Boy, both from 0xQuan, two nil. The closer was the second humans-versus-AI fight of the night: Jugbadi (bounce) ft Jaystreetz by Gesd1 against Life's Too Hard Today by S.R. Chappell, an old band recording with real vocals that he ran through Suno to add horns. Jugbadi won the chart, Chappell won the poll by a single vote, and DJ Wavy broke the tie for Chappell at 85 percent: "an emotionally resonant pop-punk anthem with superior vocal performance."

The line worth repeating came from Zaal, between battles: "Everybody argues about AI music versus human music. We bring it to the battlefield and settle it immediately."
