WaveWednesday: the $200 fight, and AI goes up 3-1

Hurricane went first on Wednesday night and nobody saw it coming, least of all Hurricane.

His song was $PEPEWavez. Across from it, WaveWarZ, the electric vibez, which Zaal made with an AI tool. Eight minutes. The chart moved like it had somewhere to be: about $200 went through it before the clock ran out, more money than Sunday's main event took across three rounds. The poll went 3 to 1 for the electric vibez. The chart agreed. Then DJ Wavy, who had the deciding vote if the first two split, made it unanimous at 80 percent confidence and read the room its homework: clean sonics, powerful vocal, dynamic arrangement.

Hurricane, once the dust settled: "This was all a shock, bro. I wasn't expecting all this calamity right off the jump." Then he thanked everyone for the chart action, because he caught some gains on the way down. That is the game in one sentence. You can lose the battle and still get paid for showing up with a song people wanted to back.

Point to Team AI. Zaal said it on air: "AI is in the lead, three to one."

Why this week, of all weeks

Every quick battle now puts a point on a board: Team AI or Team HumanZ, depending on who made the winning song. The decider is 10 January, 59 days out. And this was the week the argument got loud outside our room. Nick Cave called Suno "the Great Cancellator" in [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/music/nick-cave-says-ai-song-generators-are-indicators-of-this-nascent-technologys-demonic-potential-3975580), writing from his own [Red Hand Files](https://www.theredhandfiles.com/separating-the-art-from-the-artist/). The same week ACE Music v1 took the top spot on the [Artificial Analysis vocal leaderboard](https://artificialanalysis.ai/music/leaderboard/vocals), 1,160 to Suno v6's 1,144, a blind listening test run by people who do not care about anyone's feelings.

Zaal, between battles: "Everybody argues about AI music versus human music. We bring it to the battlefield and settle it immediately."

How a battle works, if you are new

Two songs. You back the one you think wins, and the price moves as people pile in. Three signals: the poll, the chart (who the crowd backed with money, because talk is free and trading is not), and DJ Wavy, our resident ear. Best two of three. The artists get paid out of that action the moment the battle settles, not from a sponsor.

The second humans-versus-AI fight, and the honest footnote

Late in the night S.R. Chappell asked to run an old band recording, Life's Too Hard Today, real vocals, real drums, horns he had added with Suno. Zaal's rule, said on air: a song with a human lead vocal is human, and this one, he said, would not count toward the record. It ran anyway, against Jugbadi (bounce) by Gesd1 featuring Jaystreetz. Jugbadi took the chart. Chappell took the poll by a single vote. DJ Wavy broke the tie for Chappell at 85 percent, "an emotionally resonant pop-punk anthem with superior vocal performance." A win, not a point. The board stays 3-1.

The rest of the night: Enter the Meta Within by geekmyth and StarBound by stormi split the poll one each, geekmyth took the chart, Wavy took StarBound, so it stands as a tie. Get Well (v5) beat Hidden in Plain Sight, both bennyj504, two nil. MOONZ beat Earth Boy, both 0xQuan, two nil. The room was sponsored by Lit Studio.

Sunday: the main event split every signal until it didn't

Dr. Bruce against Nessy the Rilla, best of three, about $72 backed across the night. A main event runs on three signals too, with a human judge in place of DJ Wavy. Round one, the judge went Bruce while the poll and the money went Nessy. Round two, the judge went Nessy while the poll and the money went Bruce. Round three, all three agreed on Bruce, and that was the night. Two rounds of disagreement, then a clean verdict. Watch for that shape; it is how the good ones go.

Wednesday 4 November: the confession

We opened the week by owning a mistake. The battle analytics had gone down the night before because the cache came off the leaderboards and the app tried to pull 172 battles at once. CPU to the ceiling, database locked, two recent battles missing for a bit. Fixed by the time the room opened. Two things from the room that every builder should have tattooed somewhere: never push to main before you test, and when it breaks, go back to the last commit that worked. Then the battles: Before the Applause by aporkalypse78 over Young Buck by stiloworld, Ca$ino over Original VS Replica, both lui, and Lighthouse and Ca$ino to a tie.
