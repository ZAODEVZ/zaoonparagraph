ZABAL Gamez Season 1: the whole run, start to finish

Three months, 31 workshops, 31 projects, 15 builders, six finalists, three live battles, three winners, everybody paid. That is the scoreboard. This is the story.

Press start

ZABAL Gamez is The ZAO's build season. Not a video game, the name gets everyone, we like it that way. You turn up with an idea, you build it over a summer with people around you, and at the end you put it in front of a crowd that has money on the outcome. Free. Online. Nobody asked for a CV.

Three lanes. Artist, if you make music or visuals. Builder, if you make software. Creator, if you make the writing and video that carries the other two out into the world. You choose your lane. A few people ran two.

Level 1, June: learn the controls

31 sessions, zero slide decks. People opened their actual setups on a live call and let you watch.

A leaderboard where holding something a long time beats holding a lot of it for a day, so loyalty is the multiplier. A shared AI workspace where every question the group asks makes it smarter, so the group ends up owning a memory none of them could build alone. A bounty board with one rule: post proof, get paid, no proof, no money.

All 31 are recorded and transcribed at zabalgamez.com/recordings. Slept through one in your timezone? It is waiting for you.

Level 2, July: build

31 projects from 15 people. The five I kept opening:

Proof Drop, by ghostmintops. Finish your work, it fingerprints it on your machine and hands you a receipt you can post. Anyone can check that receipt against the work later. "I made this, on this date" stops being a claim and becomes a fact.

Chroma Poker, by jdwalka. Poker inside Farcaster with a live display showing the numbers on the people you are playing while the hand runs. Real players, real hands, and bug fixes shipping between games.

Ledger, by dee-13. A manga. Five art school friends, one can forge anything, it turns into an underground operation. Read it in the browser like a flipbook. Yes, it counts.

ColorZAO, by uniquebeing404. A grey canvas. Where you paint, an independent artist's real work comes through. You do not know what it is until you have done the work. The link you send someone at 1am.

HOOD, by presdency. A marketplace where a person and an AI agent can both list and buy. Same shop, two kinds of customer. Weird for a minute, then obvious.

Boss level, August: the battles

Two finalists per lane, head to head on WaveWarZ. New to it? Two artists go up. You back the one you think wins. The price moves as people pile in. The artists get paid from that action directly, not from a sponsor.

A poll on its own is a popularity contest, so each battle ran on three signals. The poll, who the crowd said. The charts, who the crowd backed with money, because talk is free and trading is not. The judges, three people who know the lane. Best two of three.

Artist final, 24 August. n3m vs dee-13. One hour in a live Space, winner called before it closed.

Creator final, 27 August. uniquebeing404 vs presdency. Same shape, same energy.

Builder final, 29 to 30 August. I was on the judges' panel for this one with Thy Revolution and paperhandpapi. 24 hours, noon Saturday to noon Sunday. jdwalka and ghostmintops built in the open the entire time while the battle traded underneath them. A Space at the start to walk through their season. A Space at the end to show what a full day and night produced. None of us slept much. The charts say a lot of you did not either.

All six finalists were paid, champions and runners-up. That was the design. Nobody built for exposure.

Where you fit

If you build, the transcripts and the repos are public. Go dig, then enter next time.
If you make art or music, the battles were made for you. Two of the three finals were yours.
If you just like watching people make things and picking a side, you are the crowd, and the crowd decides.
