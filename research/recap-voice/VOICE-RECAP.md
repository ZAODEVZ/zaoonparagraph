# VOICE-RECAP

Last updated: 3 September 2026, by IMan. Source: six drafts of the ZABAL Gamez Season 1 recap and Zaal's notes on each one.

This is the rulebook for the recap and deep-dive lanes. If a draft breaks a rule here, the draft is wrong, not the rule. This file uses normal capitals and lists because it is a reference document, not a post. The voice rules in `VOICE.md` do not apply to structural docs.


## 1. Who is reading

Three people read every ZAO post at the same time. Write for all three in one text.

- The everyday internet reader. Not on Farcaster. Never heard of WaveWarZ. Will leave if their brain has to work. They need to get it on the first read and feel something.
- The technical reader. Wants the actual mechanics: how the battle is scored, what the tool really does, what was built. Will spot a vague sentence and trust us less for it.
- The creative and the observer. The artist, the writer, the person who just likes watching people build things. They need to feel seen, not talked past.

The middle ground is the job: always technical, never heavy. Explain the real mechanism in one plain line so the average reader does not fry their brain thinking about it.


## 2. Who is talking

A person from The ZAO. First person. Someone who was in the room.

- Not the daily-3 voice. Daily-3 is short, hard and fast, and that is right for a 400-character update. On a 1,500-word recap it reads as a wall.
- Not a company. Not "we are pleased to announce". Not a press release.
- Casual and serious at the same time, the way people talk when they care about the thing. A friend who knows the details telling you what happened.
- If the writer judged a battle, hosted a session, or stayed up for the 24 hours, say so. Being there is the credential.


## 3. Hard rules

These come from the ZABAL Gamez brand rules and apply to every public post.

- No emojis. Ever.
- No em dashes. Use a hyphen, a comma, a full stop, or a new sentence.
- No crypto, web3, onchain, token, wallet or coin vocabulary in public copy. Say "digital creators", "builders", "backed with money", "the charts", "paid".
- "100+ members" for The ZAO, never a specific number.
- Do not invent a fact, a name, a number or a date. If it is not known, write it as a placeholder in square brackets and say who has to fill it.
- Every fact comes from a source: a repo data file, a previous post, a recording, or a named person who was there.
- A future thing with no date: say it is coming and that there are no dates yet. Do not make some up to look organised.
- Open with "zm" on its own line. Close with the sign-off line the daily posts use.


## 4. The shape that worked

**Title.** Plain, specific, a little attitude. "ZABAL Gamez Season 1: the whole run, start to finish." Not "A Recap of Our Recent Event".

**Scoreboard first.** One line of the real numbers before any story. "Three months, 31 workshops, 31 projects, 15 builders, six finalists, three live battles, three winners, everybody paid. That is the scoreboard. This is the story."

**Then the story, in sections with headers that belong to the subject.** For ZABAL Gamez the headers are the arcade, because the brand is an arcade: Press start / Level 1, June / Level 2, July / Boss level, August / High scores / Where you fit / Continue? / Insert Coin. For ZAOstock the headers come from the day: the parklet, the battle block, the indoor set, doors. Never a template's headers.

**Each section.** Short paragraphs, two to four sentences, one idea each. Lists only when the items are real things: five builds, three battles, three winners. Each item gets a name, who made it, and one plain line of what it does.

**Speak to the three readers directly, once, near the end.** "If you build, the transcripts and the repos are public. Go dig, then enter next time. If you make art or music, the battles were made for you. If you just like watching people make things and picking a side, you are the crowd, and the crowd decides."

**Close.** What is next, with the one real date. Subscribe. Follow /zabal on Farcaster. Then the sign-off. No "Love, name". No "Much love to everyone". Those belong to other people's posts.


## 5. Technical but abstracted: how to explain a mechanic

Pattern: name the thing, say what it does in one everyday sentence, add the one technical detail that makes it real. Lines that passed:

- Proof Drop. "Finish your work, it fingerprints it on your machine and hands you a receipt you can post. Anyone can check that receipt against the work later."
- WaveWarZ. "Two artists go up. You back the one you think wins. The price moves as people pile in. The artists get paid from that action directly, not from a sponsor."
- Battle scoring. "A poll on its own is a popularity contest, so each battle ran on three signals. The poll, who the crowd said. The charts, who the crowd backed with money, because talk is free and trading is not. The judges, three people who know the lane. Best two of three."
- Empire Builder. "A leaderboard where holding something a long time beats holding a lot of it for a day, so loyalty is the multiplier."
- Bonfire. "A shared AI workspace where every question the group asks makes it smarter, so the group ends up owning a memory none of them could build alone."
- POIDH. "One rule: post proof, get paid. No proof, no money."

Never the abstraction without the mechanism, never the mechanism without the abstraction.


## 6. Human tells

What makes it read like a person typed it.

- A detail only someone in the room would know. "None of us slept much. The charts say a lot of you did not either." "The link you send someone at 1am."
- People by the handle they use. n3m, ghostmintops, uniquebeing404, jdwalka, dee-13, presdency. Real names in brackets only if they use them publicly.
- A small opinion. "Yes, it counts." "Weird for a minute, then obvious." "The name gets everyone, we like it that way."
- Honesty about the gaps. "No dates yet." "Not booked."
- Rhythm. A long sentence next to a very short one. Not every sentence the same length.

Reference for what a human post feels like: farcaster.xyz/rathermercurial.eth/0x1173783c, a person saying they can no longer read long AI-written documents. If a draft would make that person close the tab, it is not done.


## 7. What failed, so it is not repeated

- **Draft 1, the Paragraph AI draft.** Template phrases: "supportive crew", "sensory experiences", "took home honors", "turn ideas into reality". Placeholders left in. Verdict: too AI-generated, and sounds like it.
- **Draft 2, plain and factual.** Correct, cold. Nobody could fall for it.
- **Draft 3, the daily-3 voice stretched to 1,500 words.** Short hard lines. A wall.
- **Draft 4, "for nerds and dorks" taken literally, written about those groups.** Verdict: write for them, not about them.
- **Draft 5, another publication's farewell-post style.** Good bones, but lines were lifted. Verdict: that was a reference, not a costume. Own style only.
- **Draft 6, the one that stood.** First person from someone who judged a battle, arcade headers that belong to the brand, every mechanic in one plain line, three readers addressed by name, scoreboard first, Insert Coin last.
